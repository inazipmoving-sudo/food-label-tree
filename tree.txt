Macronutrients
 Protein
  Essential amino acids
   Histidine|Essential amino acid with an imidazole side chain|Precursor of histamine; needed for growth and tissue repair|Meat, fish, poultry, soy, cheese
   Isoleucine|Branched-chain essential amino acid (BCAA)|Muscle protein synthesis and energy|Meat, fish, eggs, cheese, lentils
   Leucine|Branched-chain essential amino acid (BCAA)|Key trigger of muscle protein synthesis (mTOR)|Beef, chicken, whey/dairy, eggs, soybeans
   Lysine|Essential amino acid often limiting in grains|Collagen cross-linking, carnitine synthesis, calcium absorption|Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, lentils, beans
   Methionine|Sulfur-containing essential amino acid|Methyl donor (via SAMe); starts protein synthesis|Eggs, fish, meat, Brazil nuts, sesame
   Phenylalanine|Aromatic essential amino acid|Precursor of tyrosine, dopamine, adrenaline|Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy
   Threonine|Essential amino acid with a hydroxyl group|Mucin, collagen and elastin production|Meat, fish, cottage cheese, lentils
   Tryptophan|Aromatic essential amino acid|Precursor of serotonin, melatonin and niacin|Turkey, chicken, eggs, milk, oats, pumpkin seeds
   Valine|Branched-chain essential amino acid (BCAA)|Muscle metabolism and energy|Meat, dairy, soy, peanuts
  Conditionally essential amino acids
   Arginine|Amino acid essential in stress/growth|Nitric oxide production, urea cycle|Peanuts, pumpkin seeds, turkey, chickpeas
   Cysteine|Sulfur amino acid made from methionine|Glutathione and keratin synthesis|Eggs, poultry, garlic, onions, oats
   Glutamine|Most abundant free amino acid in blood|Fuel for gut and immune cells|Beef, chicken, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu
   Glycine|Smallest amino acid|Collagen, glutathione, inhibitory neurotransmitter|Gelatin, bone broth, pork skin, chicken skin
   Proline|Cyclic amino acid abundant in collagen|Collagen structure and wound healing|Gelatin, bone broth, dairy (casein)
   Tyrosine|Aromatic amino acid made from phenylalanine|Precursor of dopamine, adrenaline, thyroid hormone|Cheese, chicken, fish, soy, almonds
   Selenocysteine|21st proteinogenic amino acid containing selenium|Active site of selenoproteins (antioxidant enzymes)|Brazil nuts, seafood, organ meats (within proteins)
  Non-essential amino acids
   Alanine|Simple amino acid made from pyruvate|Glucose-alanine cycle between muscle and liver|Meat, fish, eggs, dairy
   Asparagine|Amide of aspartic acid|Protein glycosylation, nervous system function|Asparagus, potatoes, dairy, meat
   Aspartic acid|Acidic amino acid|Urea cycle and neurotransmission|Soy, peanuts, meat, asparagus
   Glutamic acid|Acidic amino acid (source of umami taste)|Main excitatory neurotransmitter; nitrogen metabolism|Parmesan, tomatoes, mushrooms, soy sauce, meat
   Serine|Hydroxyl amino acid|Phospholipid and nucleotide synthesis|Eggs, soy, dairy, meat
  Protein types seen on labels
   Whey protein|Soluble milk protein fraction|Fast-digesting complete protein|Milk, ricotta, yogurt
   Casein|Main milk protein (~80%)|Slow-digesting complete protein|Milk, cheese, yogurt
   Soy protein|Protein from soybeans|Complete plant protein|Soybeans, tofu, tempeh, edamame
   Pea protein|Protein isolated from yellow peas|Plant protein rich in lysine|Yellow split peas, green peas
   Gluten|Wheat/barley/rye protein (gliadin + glutenin)|Gives dough elasticity; triggers celiac disease|Wheat, barley, rye, spelt
   Collagen / gelatin|Structural animal protein rich in glycine and proline|Connective tissue support; incomplete protein|Bone broth, pork skin, chicken skin, fish skin
   Collagen peptides|Hydrolyzed collagen|Provides glycine/proline; studied for skin and joints|Bone broth; supplements from hides/fish skin
   Bioactive peptides (e.g. carnosine, anserine)|Small peptides in meat and fish|Muscle acid buffering, antioxidant|Beef, chicken, pork, tuna
   Egg albumin|Main egg white protein|High-quality complete protein|Egg whites
  Non-proteinogenic amino acids in foods
   Ornithine|Urea-cycle amino acid not built into proteins|Ammonia removal; arginine metabolism|Cheese, fish, meat (small free amounts)
   Citrulline|Urea-cycle amino acid|Converted to arginine; nitric oxide production|Watermelon (esp. rind), cucumber, melons
   GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid)|Amino acid neurotransmitter|Inhibitory neurotransmitter; dietary effects on brain unclear|Fermented foods, tomatoes, germinated brown rice, tea (GABA tea)
   Hydroxyproline|Modified proline in collagen|Collagen stability|Gelatin, bone broth, skin
   Hydroxylysine|Modified lysine in collagen|Collagen cross-linking|Gelatin, bone broth
   Betaine (trimethylglycine)|Glycine derivative|Methyl donor; lowers homocysteine|Beets, spinach, wheat bran, quinoa
   Sarcosine (N-methylglycine)|Glycine derivative|Intermediate of choline/glycine metabolism|Egg yolks, turkey, ham, legumes
   Ergothioneine|Sulfur-containing histidine derivative|Antioxidant with its own transporter in cells|Mushrooms (esp. king oyster, porcini), oats
 Carbohydrates
  Monosaccharides
   Glucose|Simple 6-carbon sugar (dextrose)|Primary fuel for cells and brain|Grapes, honey, dates, bananas
   Fructose|Fruit sugar, 6-carbon ketose|Metabolized mainly in the liver|Apples, pears, honey, mangoes, dates
   Galactose|6-carbon sugar, half of lactose|Converted to glucose in the liver|Milk and dairy (from lactose)
   Ribose|5-carbon sugar|Backbone of RNA and ATP|All living cells (small amounts); meat, mushrooms
   Mannose|6-carbon sugar|Glycoprotein synthesis; studied for urinary tract health|Cranberries, apples, peaches (small amounts)
   Xylose|5-carbon "wood sugar"|Poorly absorbed; used to make xylitol|Corn cobs, birch, fruits (bound in hemicellulose)
   Arabinose|5-carbon sugar|May reduce sucrose digestion|Bound in pectin and hemicellulose (beets, grains)
   Tagatose|Rare 6-carbon sugar (fructose isomer)|Low-calorie sweetener|Heated dairy products (traces)
   Allulose (psicose)|Rare sugar, fructose epimer|Sweet but nearly calorie-free|Figs, raisins, jackfruit (traces)
  Disaccharides
   Sucrose|Table sugar = glucose + fructose|Quick energy|Sugar cane, sugar beets, maple syrup, pineapple, carrots
   Lactose|Milk sugar = glucose + galactose|Energy; needs lactase enzyme to digest|Milk, yogurt, soft cheese
   Maltose|Malt sugar = two glucose|Energy from starch breakdown|Sprouted grains, malt, sweet potatoes
   Trehalose|Two glucose units (mushrooms, yeast)|Slowly digested energy|Mushrooms, baker's yeast, honey
   Isomaltulose (palatinose)|Glucose + fructose (slow-digesting)|Slow, steady glucose release|Honey, sugarcane extract (traces)
   Lactulose|Galactose + fructose formed by heating milk|Not digested; laxative and prebiotic|Heat-treated milk (traces)
   Cellobiose|Two glucose units (beta-linked)|Not digested by humans|Breakdown product of cellulose; honey
   Melibiose|Galactose + glucose|Fermented by gut bacteria|Breakdown of raffinose in legumes
  Oligosaccharides
   Raffinose|Trisaccharide in beans|Fermented by gut bacteria (gas)|Beans, cabbage, broccoli, asparagus
   Stachyose|Tetrasaccharide in legumes|Prebiotic; fermented in the colon|Soybeans, chickpeas, lentils, beans
   Fructooligosaccharides (FOS)|Short fructose chains|Prebiotic fiber|Onions, garlic, leeks, bananas, asparagus
   Galactooligosaccharides (GOS)|Short galactose chains|Prebiotic; feeds bifidobacteria|Legumes, human milk
   Verbascose|Pentasaccharide in legumes|Fermented in colon (gas)|Fava beans, peas, mung beans
   Human milk oligosaccharides (2'-fucosyllactose)|Complex sugars in breast milk|Feed infant bifidobacteria|Human milk; added to infant formula
   Xylooligosaccharides (XOS)|Short xylose chains|Prebiotic|Bamboo shoots, fruits, vegetables, honey (small)
   Isomaltooligosaccharides (IMO)|Short glucose chains (alpha-1,6)|Partly digested; mild prebiotic|Miso, soy sauce, honey
  Starches
   Amylose|Linear glucose polymer in starch|Slower-digesting starch|Legumes, long-grain rice, potatoes
   Amylopectin|Branched glucose polymer in starch|Rapidly digested energy|Potatoes, sticky rice, wheat, corn
   Resistant starch|Starch that escapes small-intestine digestion|Acts like fiber; feeds gut bacteria|Green bananas, cooled potatoes/rice, beans, oats
   Maltodextrin|Partially broken-down starch|Very fast-absorbing carbohydrate|Not naturally in foods; snacks, sports drinks
   Modified starch|Chemically/physically altered starch (E1404-E1452)|Thickener; digested like starch|Not naturally in foods; sauces, soups, desserts
   Glycogen|Animal storage form of glucose|Energy reserve in liver and muscle|Liver, muscle meat, shellfish (small amounts)
   Dextrin|Partially hydrolysed starch|Digested to glucose|Toasted bread crust; added as thickener
   Resistant dextrin|Heat/acid-treated starch fiber|Soluble fiber, prebiotic|Not naturally in foods; fiber-enriched products
   Phytoglycogen|Water-soluble glycogen-like plant starch|Energy|Sweet corn
  Fiber
   Soluble fiber
    Beta-glucan|Soluble fiber in oats and barley|Lowers LDL cholesterol|Oats, barley, mushrooms
    Pectin|Soluble fiber in fruit (E440)|Gel-forming; slows digestion|Apples, citrus peel, quince, plums
    Inulin|Fructose polymer from chicory, onions|Prebiotic; feeds gut bacteria|Chicory root, Jerusalem artichoke, onions, garlic
    Psyllium|Mucilage fiber from Plantago seeds|Bulks stool; lowers cholesterol|Psyllium husk (Plantago ovata seed)
    Guar gum (as fiber)|Galactomannan from guar beans|Slows glucose absorption|Guar beans
    Glucomannan|Fiber from konjac root|Absorbs water; promotes fullness|Konjac root (shirataki noodles)
    Alginate|Fiber from brown seaweed|Forms gels in the gut|Brown seaweeds (kelp, wakame)
    Arabinoxylan|Hemicellulose fiber of cereal grains|Prebiotic; slows glucose rise|Wheat bran, rye, psyllium
    Galactomannans|Mannose-galactose polymers|Viscous fiber; lowers cholesterol|Guar, locust bean, fenugreek
    Arabinogalactan|Branched galactose-arabinose fiber|Prebiotic; immune effects studied|Larch, carrots, radish, tomatoes
    Fucoidan|Sulfated seaweed polysaccharide|Studied for immune and anticoagulant effects|Brown seaweeds (kombu, wakame)
    Mucilage|Gel-forming plant polysaccharides|Soothes gut; bulks stool|Flaxseed, chia, okra
    Polydextrose|Synthetic glucose polymer (E1200)|Soluble fiber, ~1 kcal/g|Not naturally in foods; low-sugar products
   Insoluble fiber
    Cellulose|Glucose polymer of plant cell walls|Adds stool bulk|Vegetables, fruit skins, bran, whole grains
    Hemicellulose|Mixed plant cell-wall polysaccharides|Adds bulk; partly fermented|Bran, whole grains, legumes, nuts
    Lignin|Non-carbohydrate woody polymer counted as fiber|Adds bulk; binds bile acids|Flaxseed, wheat bran, berry seeds, root vegetables
    Chitin|Fiber in mushrooms and crustacean shells|Indigestible bulk|Mushrooms, crab and shrimp shells
    Xyloglucan|Hemicellulose in plant cell walls|Adds bulk|Tamarind seed, fruits, vegetables
    Cutin / suberin|Waxy plant-surface polymers counted as fiber|Adds bulk|Fruit and vegetable skins
  Sugar alcohols (polyols)
   Sorbitol|Polyol from glucose (E420)|Low-calorie sweetener; laxative in excess|Prunes, pears, apples, peaches
   Mannitol|Polyol (E421)|Sweetener; laxative in excess|Mushrooms, cauliflower, celery
   Xylitol|5-carbon polyol (E967)|Sweetener that does not feed tooth-decay bacteria|Plums, strawberries, cauliflower (traces); birch/corn-derived
   Erythritol|4-carbon polyol (E968)|Nearly zero-calorie sweetener|Grapes, pears, melon, fermented foods (traces)
   Maltitol|Polyol from maltose (E965)|Sugar-like sweetener with fewer calories|Not naturally in significant amounts; sugar-free candy
   Isomalt|Polyol from sucrose (E953)|Sugar-free candy sweetener|Not naturally in foods; sugar-free hard candy
   Lactitol|Polyol from lactose (E966)|Low-calorie sweetener|Not naturally in foods; sugar-free desserts
