Quiz

Tap a question to see the answer

1. How many bonds does carbon make, and how many does hydrogen make?

Carbon 4, hydrogen 1, oxygen 2 (Figure 2).

2. What does "saturated" mean?

Every tail carbon holds as many hydrogens as it can, because there are no double bonds (Figure 9).

3. Why is butter solid at room temperature while olive oil is liquid?

Butter is rich in saturated fatty acids with straight chains that pack tightly. Olive oil is mostly oleic acid, whose cis double bond kinks the chain so it can't pack (Figures 12 and 14).

4. What does the "3" in omega-3 mean, and where do you start counting?

Count from the tail (ω) end, ω1 = last carbon; the first double bond starts at carbon 3 (Figure 15).

5. Which omega-3s come straight from fish or algae?

EPA and DHA. Plant ALA converts to them only poorly (Figure 15, pink box).

6. How is industrial trans fat made, and why does it behave like saturated fat?

By partial hydrogenation of vegetable oil, which flips some cis double bonds to trans (Figure 18). A trans double bond keeps the chain straight, so it packs and solidifies like saturated fat (Figures 11 and 17).

7. Name a natural source of trans fat.

Milk, butter, cheese, beef or lamb: vaccenic acid made by bacteria in cows, sheep and goats (Figure 17).

8. Do steroid hormones come from saturated fat?

No. They are made from cholesterol, which the liver can build from fat breakdown products (Figure 9, pink box).
