Epigenetic aging and transposon dysregulation reflect size-related lifespan compression in dogs

Blaise L Mariner, Brianah M McCoy, Ashlee Greenier … M Katherine Tolbert

Science · 8 Oct 2026 · Research

Read the original paper →doi:10.1126/science.aeb2986

Paywalled. The full paper needs a subscription; the abstract is free at the DOI link.

Big dogs and male dogs age faster at the DNA-methylation level, and in big dogs the change centres on 'jumping genes'.

Why it matters

Small dog breeds can live twice as long as giant breeds, which makes dogs a natural experiment in why lifespan differs within one species. This study asks whether that difference shows up in the rate of molecular aging, and where in the genome. It points to transposable elements, parts of the genome normally kept silent by methylation, as possible links between body size and lifespan. Dogs share our homes, much of our environment and many of our diseases, while living much shorter lives, so they are a useful bridge between lab animals and people.

What they did

The researchers analysed 1,640 methylomes from 894 dogs, built an epigenetic clock, and tested whether it predicted death. They compared age-related methylation at dog versions of human age-associated genes and looked at how body size and sex changed the pace and pattern of epigenetic aging.

What they found

The dog clock predicted mortality and showed that epigenetic aging is fastest early in life. At genes matching human age-associated genes, dogs showed similar age effects on promoter methylation, highlighting shared remodelling of immune pathways. Larger dogs and male dogs, which live shorter lives, showed faster molecular aging. Different parts of the genome carried these effects: sex-related changes were concentrated on the X chromosome, while size-related changes were especially strong at transposable elements. The authors conclude that epigenetics reflects lifespan differences in dogs and identify transposable elements as possible mediators of the size effect.

Limits

The study shows associations between size, methylation and lifespan; it does not prove that transposable-element changes shorten life. Results in dogs may not carry over to people. Only the abstract was available to us; the full text is behind a paywall. Size and sex are tied up with many other differences between dogs, such as breed history, growth rate and neutering, which an observational study cannot fully separate. The findings describe groups of dogs, not individual animals.

Key terms

Methylome
A map of DNA methylation across the genome.
Transposable element
A stretch of DNA that can copy or move itself; normally silenced by methylation.
Promoter
The DNA region at the start of a gene that controls whether it is switched on.
Epigenetic clock
A formula that turns DNA methylation into an estimate of biological age.
Ortholog
The version of the same gene in another species.
X chromosome
A sex chromosome; females have two copies and males one.
Mortality prediction
Testing whether a measure can forecast who dies sooner during follow-up.
