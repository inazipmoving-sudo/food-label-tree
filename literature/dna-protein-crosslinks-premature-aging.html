DNA-protein cross-links promote cGAS-STING-driven premature aging and embryonic lethality

Ines Tomaskovic, Cristian Prieto-Garcia, Maria Boskovic … Ivan Dikic

Science · 29 Jan 2026 · Research

← Back to the Longevity course

Read the original paper →doi:10.1126/science.adx9445

Open access. The full paper is free to read (free copy).

Unrepaired DNA-protein tangles leak DNA into the cell, set off an immune alarm, and cause premature aging in mice; blocking the alarm helps.

Why it matters

DNA damage is a central hallmark of aging, but it comes in many forms, and some of them are poorly studied. DNA-protein cross-links, in which a protein becomes chemically stuck to DNA, are among the most toxic. This study shows how one specific repair failure can turn into body-wide inflammation and early aging, and it points to a pathway that drugs could target. It also links DNA damage to "inflammaging", the chronic inflammation that rises with age. Rare 'progeroid' diseases, in which people age early because one repair system is broken, have long been used to find out which kinds of DNA damage matter most for aging.

What they did

The researchers studied SPRTN, an enzyme that cuts away proteins stuck to DNA. Working in cells, they asked what happens when SPRTN fails during DNA copying and during cell division. They then made mice carrying a Sprtn mutation that models Ruijs-Aalfs syndrome, a rare human premature-aging disease, and tested whether blocking the cGAS-STING immune pathway, genetically or with a drug, changed the outcome.

What they found

Without working SPRTN, cells were left with unresolved DNA damage during both DNA copying and cell division. Chromosomes separated incorrectly, micronuclei formed, and DNA spilled into the cytoplasm, where it switched on the cGAS-STING pathway, a sensor that normally detects viral DNA. In the mutant mice, chronic cGAS-STING signalling caused inflammation and innate immune responses that killed many embryos. Mice that survived showed signs of aging that began before birth and continued into adulthood. Blocking cGAS-STING genetically or with a drug rescued the embryos and eased the premature-aging features.

Limits

This is a mouse model of a rare inherited disease, so it shows what happens when one repair enzyme is badly impaired, not necessarily what drives normal human aging. The abstract does not say whether the same lesions build up enough in ordinary aging to matter. Drugs that block cGAS-STING may carry risks of their own, because the pathway also defends against infection.

Key terms

DNA-protein cross-link (DPC)
A protein stuck to DNA by a chemical bond, which blocks copying and reading of the gene.
SPRTN
A protease enzyme that chops up proteins trapped on DNA so the DNA can be repaired.
Micronucleus
A small, separate packet of chromosome material that forms after faulty cell division.
cGAS-STING
An immune sensor that detects DNA where it should not be (in the cytoplasm) and triggers inflammation.
Progeroid
Showing features that look like premature aging.
Ruijs-Aalfs syndrome
A rare inherited disease caused by SPRTN mutations, with premature aging and early liver cancer.
